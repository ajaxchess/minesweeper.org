Flag Value — Pattern Guide

Level 6 · Find the provable mine whose flag unlocks the most new cells.

Level 6 combines flagging and chording. The board has several hidden cells that are provably mines — but not every flag is equally useful. Some flags unlock a big chord; others enable nothing at all. Your job is to identify which provable mine is worth flagging right now, based on how much territory it opens.

Step 1 Find the provable mines — same as Level 3

A provable mine is a hidden cell where the numbers force it to be a mine — a revealed number whose unrevealed-unflagged neighbor count exactly equals its remaining mine quota. All those neighbors must be mines.

Identify candidates

  1. Find a revealed number with remaining quota N.
  2. Count its unflagged hidden neighbors.
  3. If that count equals N, every hidden neighbor is a provable mine — a flag candidate.
  4. Repeat for all revealed numbers to build your candidate list.
💡 Level 6 always has multiple provable mines. Your task isn't just to flag one — it's to flag the right one.

Step 2 For each candidate, count how many cells its flag would enable

A flag's value is determined by what chords it enables after being placed. Mentally flag each candidate mine and ask: which revealed numbers become chord-ready? How many cells do those chords reveal?

Score each candidate

  1. For each provable mine, mentally place the flag.
  2. Find revealed numbers adjacent to that flag that now become chord-ready (their flag count equals their value).
  3. For each newly chord-ready number, count its hidden non-flag neighbors — that's the chord's reveal.
  4. Sum all reveals: that's the flag's total value.
💡 A flag on a cell surrounded by already-satisfied numbers or fully-revealed neighbors has zero value — it earns no chord. Look for flags whose adjacent numbers are one flag away from being chord-ready.

Level 6 rewards planning one step ahead: the mine you flag now determines what you can chord next. Think flag → chord as a single two-step move.
